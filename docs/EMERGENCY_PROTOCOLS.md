# ASTRA: Emergency Life-Support Protocols

> **Demo content only:** The workflows below are fictional examples, not medically approved protocols or instructions for real emergencies. They have not been reviewed by qualified clinicians. Do not use them for patient care.

ASTRA includes 10 illustrative emergency workflows accessible through the Emergency Response Console.

---

## 1. `PROT-01` — Cabin Hypoxia & Suspected Decompression Sickness
- **Category**: Environmental / Respiratory
- **Observable Warning Signs**: $\text{SpO}_2 < 90\%$, cyanosis, cognitive confusion, tachypnea, joint bends pain, cabin pressure drop.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Don Portable Breathing Apparatus (PBA) with 100% $\text{O}_2$. Verify habitat hatch seal.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Connect crew member to 100% normobaric oxygen via high-flow mask.
  2. Recline patient to 30 degrees and restrict movement.
  3. Increase habitat/suit pressure if decompression occurred.
  4. Administer oral fluids if conscious and airway secure.
  5. Continuously record $\text{SpO}_2$, heart rate, and respiratory rate.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT allow active physical exertion. DO NOT delay oxygen administration while troubleshooting cabin sensors.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Failure of $\text{SpO}_2$ to rise above 92% within 3 minutes of 100% $\text{O}_2$, or acute focal neurological deficits.

## 2. `PROT-02` — Acute Cardiac Episode & Severe Chest Pain
- **Category**: Cardiovascular
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Substernal crushing chest pressure, radiation to left arm/jaw, diaphoresis, sudden malignant arrhythmia.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Halt physical exertion immediately. Seat patient in semi-Fowler's posture. Ensure automated external defibrillator (AED) is on standby.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Position crew member upright or semi-recumbent; minimize physical exertion.
  2. Apply 3-lead ECG / pulse oximeter for continuous monitoring.
  3. Provide supplemental oxygen if $\text{SpO}_2 < 94\%$.
  4. Administer 325 mg chewable Aspirin if no allergy or active hemorrhage.
  5. Prepare sublingual Nitroglycerin (0.4 mg) if systolic BP $> 100\text{ mmHg}$.
  6. Transmit priority telemetry packet to Flight Surgeon.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT administer Nitroglycerin if systolic BP $< 100\text{ mmHg}$ or if PDE5 inhibitors used within 48h.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Persistent pain $> 10$ minutes, hemodynamic collapse (BP $< 90/60$), or loss of responsiveness.

## 3. `PROT-03` — Loss of Consciousness & Orthostatic Collapse
- **Category**: Neurological / Cardiovascular
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Complete unresponsiveness, absent verbal response, sudden collapse, transient pallor, weak peripheral pulse.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Clear immediate surroundings in microgravity to prevent traumatic collision. Check Airway, Breathing, Circulation (ABC).
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Secure patient in crew restraint to prevent uncontrolled drift.
  2. Open airway using head-tilt chin-lift (jaw-thrust if trauma suspected).
  3. Confirm carotid/radial pulse and spontaneous respirations.
  4. Elevate lower extremities to facilitate venous return.
  5. Check fingerstick blood glucose and $\text{SpO}_2$.
  6. Maintain patient in lateral recovery posture if breathing resumes but consciousness remains blunted.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT administer oral fluids to an unconscious crew member.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Unresponsiveness lasting $> 2$ minutes, focal seizure activity, or trauma sustained during fall/drift.

## 4. `PROT-04` — Severe Traumatic Hemorrhage & Laceration
- **Category**: Trauma
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Pulsatile or copious blood loss, spreading hematoma, signs of hypovolemic shock (tachycardia $> 110$, hypotension).
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Don sterile PPE. In microgravity, airborne blood droplets foul air circulation; activate suction and absorbent gauze.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Apply direct firm manual pressure over bleeding site with sterile hemostatic gauze.
  2. If limb arterial bleeding is uncontrolled by direct pressure, immediately apply Emergency Tourniquet 5-7 cm proximal to wound.
  3. Tighten tourniquet until bleeding halts and distal pulse is abolished; record application time.
  4. Establish IV access and prepare isotonic crystalloid infusion if shock manifests.
  5. Capture airborne fluid droplets with absorbent pads.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT apply tourniquet over a joint. DO NOT loosen an applied tourniquet without direct Flight Surgeon authorization.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Inability to control bleeding with direct pressure/tourniquet, systolic BP $< 90\text{ mmHg}$, or tourniquet in place $> 60$ minutes.

## 5. `PROT-05` — Acute Solar Particle Event / Severe Radiation Exposure
- **Category**: Environmental / Radiation
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Dosimeter alarm $> 100\text{ }\mu\text{Sv/h}$, external coronal mass ejection alert, nausea, vomiting, erythema.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: IMMEDIATE EVACUATION to the Storm Shelter (central water-shielded habitat core). Seal radiation hatches.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Sound habitat radiation alarm; all crew report to Storm Shelter within 3 minutes.
  2. Verify storm shelter water-wall shielding integrity.
  3. Log personal dosimeter readings for each astronaut.
  4. Administer antiemetics (Ondansetron 8 mg IV/oral) at onset of nausea.
  5. Prepare Granulocyte Colony-Stimulating Factor (G-CSF) protocol if cumulative dose exceeds 1.0 Gy.
  6. Maintain continuous communication with Earth Solar Physics & Flight Surgery teams.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT permit any EVA or unshielded module access until Ground declares event terminated.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Cumulative personal dose $> 500\text{ mSv}$ within 24 hours, persistent vomiting, or severe leukopenia.

## 6. `PROT-06` — Toxic Atmospheric Contamination / Smoke
- **Category**: Environmental / Toxicology
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Trace contaminant analyzer alarm, pungent chemical odor, eye lacrimation, severe coughing, bronchospasm.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: IMMEDIATE donning of Quick-Don Mask with Carbon/HEPA scrubber canister or Emergency PBA. Isolate affected module.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Sound cabin atmospheric contamination alarm; isolate the contaminated module hatch.
  2. All crew don emergency breathing apparatus.
  3. Activate high-capacity ECLSS trace contaminant scrubbers.
  4. Rinse exposed eyes/skin with copious saline from emergency wash bottles.
  5. Administer Albuterol / Salbutamol inhaler (4 puffs via spacer) for bronchospasm.
  6. Monitor exhaled CO and cabin hazardous gas concentration levels.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT open isolated module hatches until atmospheric sample tests confirm toxic agents are below permissible exposure limits (SMAC).
- **Escalation Criteria**: Persistent respiratory distress, refractory bronchospasm, or corneal chemical burns.

## 7. `PROT-07` — Severe Space Motion Sickness & Vestibular Ataxia
- **Category**: Neuro-Vestibular
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Intractable vomiting, severe vertigo, spatial disorientation, inability to coordinate hand-eye tasks, pallor, cold sweat.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Fasten astronaut into crew restraint berth to prevent disorientation and injury during vehicle maneuvers.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Restrict head and neck movements; avoid sudden rotational pitch/yaw motions.
  2. Administer Promethazine (25-50 mg IM/oral) or Scopolamine transdermal patch as prescribed.
  3. Provide oral electrolyte rehydration solution in small, frequent sips to prevent volume depletion.
  4. Keep ambient cabin illumination subdued; minimize visual clutter.
  5. Monitor for signs of secondary dehydration and electrolyte imbalance.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT assign safety-critical piloting, robotic arm manipulation, or EVA duties while symptomatic or sedated.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Inability to retain liquids $> 24$ hours, signs of orthostatic collapse, or persistent vomiting unresponsive to antiemetics.

## 8. `PROT-08` — Acute Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-Ocular Syndrome (SANS)
- **Category**: Neuro-Ocular
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Progressive visual blurring, scotoma, severe retro-orbital headache, optic disc edema (papilledema), cotton-wool spots.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Dim crew workstation screens. Discontinue prolonged high-concentration close-up visual tasks.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Conduct comprehensive funduscopic and visual acuity assessment via onboard ophthalmic diagnostic tools.
  2. Measure intraocular pressure (IOP) using tonometer.
  3. Evaluate neurological status and cranial nerve function.
  4. Apply lower-body negative pressure (LBNP) protocol if approved by Flight Surgeon to redistribute cephalad fluid shifts.
  5. Adjust sleep head-tilt angle and monitor cabin $\text{CO}_2$ levels (ensure $\text{CO}_2 < 2.0\text{ mmHg}$).
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT perform high-intensity Valsalva-inducing resistance exercise until neuro-ophthalmologist evaluation.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Sudden visual acuity loss $> 2$ lines on Snellen chart, visual field deficit, or intractable headache accompanied by nausea.

## 9. `PROT-09` — Acute Environmental Hypothermia
- **Category**: Environmental / Thermal
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Core body temperature $< 35.0^\circ\text{C}$, uncontrolled shivering, slurred speech, apathy, peripheral cyanosis, bradycardia.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Remove damp garments. Wrap patient in aluminized reflective thermal space blanket within insulated sleeping bag.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Insulate patient from cold metal surfaces and thermal bridging in spacecraft hull.
  2. Apply warm packs (chemically activated) to groin, axillae, and torso.
  3. Administer warm, sweetened oral fluids if patient is conscious and swallow reflex is intact.
  4. Warm cabin environmental temperature zone to 24-26°C.
  5. Monitor core temperature continuously via tympanic/axillary sensor.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT rub frostbitten or cold extremities. DO NOT apply direct scalding heat sources to peripheral limbs.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Core temperature $< 34.0^\circ\text{C}$, cessation of shivering, cardiac dysrhythmias, or deteriorating level of consciousness.

## 10. `PROT-10` — Severe Dehydration & Heat Strain Syndrome
- **Category**: Metabolic / Environmental
- **Observable Warning Signs**: Tachycardia $> 115\text{ bpm}$ at rest, dark concentrated urine, skin turgor loss, sunken eyes, orthostatic dizziness, elevated core temp $> 38.5^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Immediate Crew Safety**: Cease all physical workouts immediately. Move to high-airflow, ventilated cabin sector.
- **First-Aid Steps**:
  1. Initiate oral rehydration therapy with WHO-formula oral rehydration salts (ORS) in potable water (1 liter over 2 hours).
  2. If oral intake is not tolerated due to nausea, establish IV line and infuse 0.9% Normal Saline at 150 mL/h.
  3. Active evaporative cooling with moist towels and localized fan airflow.
  4. Measure urine specific gravity and volume output.
  5. Re-check vital signs every 15 minutes until hemodynamic stability is restored.
- **Contraindications**: DO NOT administer cold bolus water too rapidly to avoid water intoxication or gastric distress.
- **Escalation Criteria**: Signs of acute heat stroke (core temp $> 40^\circ\text{C}$, altered mental status, delirium, anhidrosis) or persistent hypotension (systolic BP $< 85\text{ mmHg}$).
